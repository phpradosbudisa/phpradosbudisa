How I'd price a SaaS today

5 deliberate pricing calls, with public numbers.

Pricing is a product decision with public consequences. I prefer numbers on the page, a clear unit of value, and tradeoffs I can explain without a spreadsheet apology.

The story in 3 steps

  1. Charge from day one with a clear unit

    Situation Free signups grow but revenue and intent stay fuzzy.

    Decision Publish price before the first signup and tie it to the action users value.

    Tradeoff Free forever maximizes top of funnel; it delays learning who will pay.

    What I choose Public price, free only as a bounded phase. On a marketplace, the unit is often the reply or the job, not the profile.

    What happens in prod Without a price on the page, every roadmap debate mixes growth with monetization.

  2. Fixed scope over open-ended meters

    Situation Clients ask for hourly support on a launch engagement.

    Decision Fix price after a small paid discovery step.

    Tradeoff Fixed price shifts overrun risk to the studio.

    What I choose A small audit credited toward a fixed sprint band; ongoing care as a flat monthly cancel-anytime line.

    What happens in prod Hourly billing rewards slow discovery; fixed pricing forces honest estimates up front.

    • convertixdigital.comConvertix pricing

  3. Commitment as a choice

    Situation Annual-only contracts boost cash but anger users who churn early.

    Decision Offer monthly and yearly with a reason to stay, not a trap.

    Tradeoff Yearly discounts reduce MRR per seat.

    What I choose Monthly and yearly side by side; reward yearly with time, not lock-in clauses.

    What happens in prod Renewals that must be earned produce fewer angry chargebacks than forced terms.

If I were pricing a new SaaS today, I'd make the same 5 calls I've made for my own products. Public numbers only.

What breaks in prod: free with no end date and no price on the page. Signups grow, revenue doesn't, and you can't tell who's serious.

My call: I give up early revenue, the upside of per-lead fees, and forced annual contracts. Providers get a price they can trust, and every renewal has to be earned. I'd make that trade again.
