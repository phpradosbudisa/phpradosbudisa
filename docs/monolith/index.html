Monolith first. Still my call.

When one deployable is right, and the 4 signals that justify a split.

Microservices are a scaling strategy, not a starting gift. I still begin with one deployable, hard module boundaries, and evidence before a split.

The story in 3 steps

  1. Name what you are optimizing for

    Situation The roadmap shows eight boxes on a diagram before the first paying user.

    Decision Ship one deployable or pay the tax of distributed operations on day one.

    Tradeoff A monolith scales as a unit; a bad deploy takes everything down.

    What I choose One app, one database, modules that own their tables and talk through interfaces.

    What happens in prod Teams that split early often debug network traces before they debug product fit.

    • martinfowler.comMonolithFirst

  2. What the monolith buys

    Situation Features need consistent transactions and fast refactors.

    Decision Keep shared state in one place until measured pain says otherwise.

    Tradeoff You cannot scale one hot endpoint without scaling the rest.

    What I choose One log stream, real transactions, refactors the test suite can follow, rollback in one step.

    What happens in prod Incident response stays in one repository until profiling proves a hotspot.

    • martinfowler.comMonolithFirst
    • martinfowler.comMicroservice Prerequisites

  3. When a split is justified

    Situation Traffic grows and one subsystem needs a different release cadence or compliance boundary.

    Decision Split on evidence from incidents and profiles, not on a slide.

    Tradeoff Services add deployment, tracing, and failure modes you previously avoided.

    What I choose Extract only when scaling profile, team boundary, isolation need, or measured pain clears the bar. Queue heavy work from day one so the boundary can move later.

    What happens in prod Partial splits without queues become the worst of both models.
