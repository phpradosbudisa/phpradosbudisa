3am Deploy

Backups are not restores

A monthly restore drill you can run in an hour.

From the post on 3 Nov 2026

Every team I have worked with that lost data had backup jobs reporting success. The gap is almost always restore: nobody timed it, and nobody tested the app against the copy.

The story in 3 steps

  1. Define loss and downtime you can accept

    Situation Backups run nightly but nobody has written down RPO or RTO.

    Decision Agree how much data and how many hours you can lose before the business cares.

    Tradeoff Tighter numbers cost storage and engineering time.

    What I choose Write RPO and RTO on the runbook wall. Match backup frequency and restore target to those numbers.

    What happens in prod During an outage, arguments about acceptable loss waste the hours you needed for the restore.

    • sre.googleGoogle SRE: Data Integrity

  2. Backup the whole system, not just the database

    Situation The database dump restores cleanly but uploads and config live elsewhere.

    Decision List every store: database, files, secrets, search indexes if any.

    Tradeoff Object storage versioning and separate secret vaults add ops work.

    What I choose Database plus files plus config in a vault. Credentials for backups live off the machine being backed up.

    What happens in prod Schema without roles, or uploads without objects, produces a green restore that still cannot serve users.

    • postgresql.orgPostgreSQL backup
    • postgresql.orgPostgreSQL PITR

  3. Restore into scratch, on a calendar

    Situation The team trusts the backup vendor dashboard.

    Decision Treat untested backup as a hypothesis.

    Tradeoff Monthly drills interrupt other work.

    What I choose Once a month, restore to a scratch environment, time it, smoke-test login and one workflow, alert on missed drills.

    What happens in prod The first real restore mid-outage is when you learn the dump never included extensions or queue state.
