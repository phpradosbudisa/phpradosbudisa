7 security holes I find in almost every audit

Check your app for the 7 holes before someone else does.

Security audits on pre-launch apps converge on the same seven gaps regardless of framework. They are boring until someone automates the boring attack.

The story in 4 steps

  1. Secrets and the browser bundle

    Situation Third-party keys sit in environment variables wired to the client build.

    Decision Decide what may ever ship to the browser.

    Tradeoff Moving calls server-side adds latency and code.

    What I choose Payment secret keys and service roles stay server-side; rotate anything that was ever client-visible.

    What happens in prod Scrapers find keys in minutes once the app is public.

    • owasp.orgOWASP Top 10

  2. Row-level access

    Situation Logged-in users see only their rows in the UI.

    Decision Enforce ownership in the database or API, not in the template.

    Tradeoff Strict rules break quick demos until policies are written properly.

    What I choose Deny by default, allow per owner, test with two ordinary accounts.

    What happens in prod Changing an ID in the query string still works when the API trusts the request.

    • supabase.comSupabase RLS
    • owasp.orgOWASP Top 10

  3. Auth endpoints and money webhooks

    Situation Login and reset endpoints accept unlimited tries; webhooks accept any JSON.

    Decision Rate-limit identity flows and verify payment signatures.

    Tradeoff Stricter limits can block legitimate users behind shared IPs.

    What I choose Limits per IP and per account; webhook handlers idempotent with signature verification.

    What happens in prod Surprise SMS bills and fake paid states are launch-week patterns, not year-two bugs.

    • cheatsheetseries.owasp.orgAuthentication Cheat Sheet
    • docs.stripe.comStripe webhook verification
    • owasp.orgOWASP API Security Top 10

  4. Storage and production hygiene

    Situation Upload buckets are public for convenience; staging debug routes ship in prod builds.

    Decision Private by default, signed URLs, type and size checks.

    Tradeoff Signed URLs expire and need refresh logic.

    What I choose No listable buckets, no debug routes or permissive CORS in production builds.

    What happens in prod Guessable file URLs and verbose errors leak more than scanners need.
