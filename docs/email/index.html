Your emails land in spam. Fix 3 records.

SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up correctly, plus the Gmail/Yahoo rules.

Transactional email fails quietly: the app thinks it sent, the user never sees it, and the code path looked fine in staging.

The story in 4 steps

  1. Separate app mail from marketing

    Situation Product and newsletter share one domain and one reputation.

    Decision Put transactional mail on a subdomain and protect its reputation.

    Tradeoff More DNS records and provider setup before launch.

    What I choose App mail from mail.example.com; marketing on its own stream with unsubscribe on bulk mail.

    What happens in prod One bad campaign can push password resets into spam for everyone on the domain.

    • support.google.comGoogle sender guidelines
    • senders.yahooinc.comYahoo sender best practices

  2. SPF and DKIM without breaking DNS

    Situation Multiple tools append TXT records and someone adds a second SPF line.

    Decision Publish one SPF record and sign with DKIM at 2048-bit where offered.

    Tradeoff SPF lookup limits cap how many includes you can chain.

    What I choose Single SPF, provider include, DKIM published and verified before go-live.

    What happens in prod Two SPF records fail authentication for every message until DNS is fixed.

    • rfc-editor.orgRFC 7208 (SPF)

  3. DMARC in phases

    Situation Receivers start rejecting mail that fails alignment.

    Decision Roll DMARC from monitor to enforce without blocking your own invoices.

    Tradeoff p=reject protects the domain; misconfiguration blocks legitimate mail.

    What I choose Start at p=none with reports, read them for two to four weeks, then quarantine, then reject.

    What happens in prod Jumping straight to reject without reports is how teams lock themselves out of their own domain.

    • rfc-editor.orgRFC 7489 (DMARC)
    • support.google.comGoogle sender guidelines

  4. Verify before launch day

    Situation DNS propagates slowly and launch is tomorrow morning.

    Decision Send test messages and read Gmail headers the week before.

    Tradeoff Waiting a week delays other work.

    What I choose Show original in Gmail: spf=pass, dkim=pass, dmarc=pass. Meet bulk sender rules if volume applies.

    What happens in prod Morning-of DNS changes are the ones that miss propagation when users arrive.

    • support.google.comGoogle sender guidelines
    • rfc-editor.orgRFC 8058 (one-click unsubscribe)
